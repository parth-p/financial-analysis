# Risks — Analyst's Checklist

*Companion to the Business Model (v2), Industry Structure, Moat, Management, Financials, and Valuation checklists. Step 7 of the framework.*

> **The one rule for this step:** **risk is the probability of permanent loss of capital — a nameable way the business or your reasoning is wrong — not volatility.** Price wobble on a business you've valued correctly isn't risk; it's your entry point. Real risk is narrower: impairment you don't recover from.
>
> **Every risk must be falsifiable and monitorable — never a vague anxiety.** "Competition" is a feeling. "Competitor X at 30% lower price takes the anchor customer within 18 months" is a risk. If you can't state what you'd *observe* if it were materializing, you haven't found one.
>
> **Two kinds of risk — only one is yours.** Risk to the *business* vs. risk to *your thesis*. A business risk already priced in (**Step 6**) is not a risk to your *return* — the market discounted it. Your risk is the **delta between what you believe and what's priced.** (This is why Step 7 needs Step 6: the same risk is irrelevant at one price and fatal at another.)

### The flag convention (same as before)
- **Format:** `Fact → Step N`
- ✅ "Thesis-broken kill trigger: DSO > 120 days for 2 quarters → Step 8 (sell discipline)"

**Who owns which judgment:** the margin of safety that *pays* for these risks → **Step 6** (revisit it) · the thesis, catalyst, time horizon, and thesis-*achieved* exit → **Step 8**.

**Incoming flags to resolve here:** moat-erosion vectors (Step 3) · solvency/refinancing & forensic red flags (Step 5) · governance/integrity/pledging (Step 4) · industry disruption & policy dependence (Step 2) · concentration (Step 1) · the embedded expectations most likely to break (Step 6).

**How to use this:** invert (assume you're wrong), permanent vs. temporary is the master cut, focus on thesis risk. Output is a *ranking* of the 2–3 risks that matter + kill criteria — not a list.

---

## 1. Reframe risk correctly (the definitional gate)
- [ ] Two questions govern everything: **how likely am I to be wrong**, and **how much do I lose permanently if I am**?
- [ ] Exclude volatility-that-recovers — it isn't on this list.
- [ ] Every entry below must be specific enough to *observe* if it started happening.

---

## 2. Business risks — *the taxonomy* (sweep every bucket)
The risk that kills you is usually the one you didn't look for. Enumerate across:
- [ ] **Competitive / disruption** — entrants, substitutes, tech & business-model shift, moat erosion (Steps 2–3).
- [ ] **Financial / balance-sheet** — leverage, refinancing, liquidity, covenants, capital-markets dependence (Step 5). *Weight heavily — the most common cause of **permanent** loss (forced selling, equity wipeout).*
- [ ] **Operational** — execution, key-person, supply chain, concentration (Step 1).
- [ ] **Regulatory / legal / political** — policy reversal, litigation, licensing, tax (Steps 2, 4).
- [ ] **Governance / integrity** — fraud, related-party, misallocation, promoter pledging (Step 4).
- [ ] **Cyclical / macro** — demand cycle, commodities, rates, FX.
- [ ] **Tail / exogenous** — low-probability, high-severity events.

---

## 3. Thesis risks — *what you're betting on* (the neglected half)
- [ ] List the load-bearing assumptions from Steps 1–6 (moat holds / margins expand / key driver grows / management allocates well / multiple re-rates).
- [ ] For each: **what would prove it wrong?**
- [ ] **The priced-in filter:** which risks does the market *already* worry about (not yours) vs. which does your thesis *uniquely* depend on (entirely yours)?
- [ ] Turn the variant perception on itself: the thing you believe that others don't is the thing you're most likely wrong about. Interrogate it hardest.

---

## 4. The pre-mortem — *assume it already failed*
- [ ] Imagine it's 3–5 years out and the investment lost money. **Write the story of what happened.** (Prospective hindsight catches what the taxonomy misses.)
- [ ] Then: what early signals would have warned you?

---

## 5. Permanence & magnitude — *the master cut*
- [ ] For each risk: **permanent impairment or temporary noise?** Recoverable or terminal?
- [ ] Weight by **probability × severity.**
- [ ] Separate the **existential** (kills the company / permanent loss) from the **drawdown** (noise — often opportunity on a business you've valued right).

*Most items on a typical risk list are noise in the costume of danger. Find the 2–3 that aren't.*

---

## 6. Correlation & hidden linkages
- [ ] Are the risks independent or **correlated**? (A recession hits demand, financing, and customer solvency at once.)
- [ ] Find the **single points of failure that appear under multiple categories** (one anchor customer = revenue + cash-flow + covenant risk). Those are the real fragilities.

---

## 7. Disconfirming evidence & kill criteria — *make it monitorable*
- [ ] Convert the top risks into a **watchlist** of observable tripwires (rising DSO, share loss, covenant threshold, competitor launch, regulatory filing).
- [ ] Define **kill criteria** *in advance*: what specifically would make you admit the thesis is broken and sell?

**Boundary:** this is the *thesis-broken* exit. The *thesis-achieved* exit and time horizon → **Step 8**. Writing kill criteria while calm and unanchored is the best defense against rationalizing down a falling stock.

---

## Traps specific to this step (re-read before finishing)
- [ ] Vague anxiety instead of falsifiable risks.
- [ ] **Risk theater** — a 10-K-style list of 40 risks, none prioritized (the opposite failure of vagueness).
- [ ] Confirmation bias — under-weighting risks to a thesis you like (pre-mortem is the antidote).
- [ ] Recency/salience bias — obsessing over the last crisis, ignoring slow risks like moat erosion.
- [ ] Treating correlated risks as independent.
- [ ] Base-rate neglect — turnarounds rarely turn; acquirers rarely add value.
- [ ] Ignoring the risk that *your own thesis* is simply wrong.

---

## The risk verdict (do this last)
State it as a *ranking*, not a list — the analog of the earlier verdict tests:

> **The 2–3 risks that actually matter:**
> 1. ______________ — probability [ ], severity [ ], **[permanent / temporary]**.
> 2. ______________ — probability [ ], severity [ ], **[permanent / temporary]**.
> **Pre-mortem — most likely way this fails:** ________________________.
> **Kill criteria (thesis broken if):** ________________________.
> **Implication for margin of safety / position size:** ________________________.

If your output is a long undifferentiated list, or nothing is marked permanent, you did risk theater. Go back to Section 5 and rank.

---

## Handoff to the rest of the framework
Step 7 produces a ranking + kill criteria. Everything tagged `→ Step N` collects here:

- **→ Step 6 (revisit):** risk and margin of safety are the same coin. A fat left tail either widens the discount you demand or shrinks the position — feed the existential risks back into whether the price pays you.
- **→ Step 8 (thesis, catalyst, horizon):** the kill criteria (thesis-broken exit); the pre-mortem as the honest counter-case the thesis must survive; whether the risks are underwritable within your horizon or disqualifying.

*Steps 1–6 built and priced the thesis. Step 7 asked how it dies, whether you'd see it coming, and whether the price pays you to take the bet.*
