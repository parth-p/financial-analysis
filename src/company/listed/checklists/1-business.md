# Business Analysis — Analyst's Checklist

*Stage 1 of the 4-Stage Equity Research Framework.*

> **The Mandate for Stage 1:** Map the machine, the arena, and the structural moat. Understand what the business physically does, who pays, who can squeeze whom, what statutory and physical limits constrain it, and why competitors cannot easily steal its economics.
>
> **The Epistemological Discipline:**
> - **Describe the structural engine — do not run financial ratio analysis.** Operating cash flow conversion, ROIC vs. WACC calculations, debt service multiples, and valuation multiples belong strictly in **Stage 3 (Financials)**. Here, describe the *operational mechanics* (e.g., "the company extends 90-day credit to OEMs while paying steel suppliers in 30 days"), not the ledger arithmetic.
> - **The Moat is a Hypothesis in Stage 1.** Map the structural source of competitive advantage (switching costs, tooling lock-in, regulatory approvals) and formulate explicit **falsification conditions** for Stage 3 to verify or refute in the numbers.

---

### The Flag Convention
When you encounter a fact that requires verification or judgment in later stages, tag it inline:
- **Format:** `Fact → Stage N (Action required)`
- ✅ *"Company relies on 51%-owned subsidiary for 100% of its high-margin chiller manufacturing → Stage 2 (check minority interest governance) & Stage 3 (track minority leakage in PAT)."*
- ✅ *"Raw material pass-through operates with a 90-day lag → Stage 3 (verify quarterly gross margin volatility during commodity spikes)."*

## 1. What the Company Does (Core Offering & Value Exchange)

*Consolidate the offering in one cohesive pass. Avoid splitting this into repetitive micro-questions.*

- [ ] **The Offering Matrix:** Complete the table below for all material revenue lines:
  | Segment / Product Line | Physical Product / Service | Concrete End Application | % of Revenue | Gross Margin / Spread Profile | Pricing Basis (Fixed / Cost-Plus / Pass-Through / L1 Tender) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
- [ ] **The "Who Gets Fired?" Test (Customer Criticality):**
  - If this product/service fails in the field or delivery halts, who at the customer organization faces severe operational, financial, or career damage?
  - *Extreme Asymmetry:* Does the component cost <1% to 3% of the customer's total equipment or operating bill of materials (BOM), while carrying catastrophic failure risk (plant downtime, explosion, safety recall, regulatory shutdown)?
  - *Low Asymmetry:* Is it a commoditized consumable where failure merely causes minor inconvenience or can be substituted on hours' notice?
- [ ] **What the Company Sells vs. What the Customer Buys:**
  - What is physically shipped on the invoice (e.g., 200L barrels of liquid hydrocarbons; DIN 2093 stamped washers; 20-meter fabric rolls)?
  - What economic outcome is the customer actually purchasing (e.g., zero substation explosions for 25 years; eliminating turbine yaw brake failure; passing garment shrinkage inspection)?
- [ ] **Real Substitutes & Non-Direct Competition:**
  - If not buying from direct industry peers, how else can the customer solve this problem?
  - Can the customer solve it in-house (captive fabrication, DIY chemical dosing, hiring junior analysts)?
  - Is there an alternative technology or material that eliminates this product entirely (e.g., circular knitting replacing woven fabric, direct liquid immersion replacing fans, EV powertrains eliminating engine oil)?

## 2. Corporate, Group & Subsidiary Architecture

*Map the legal reality. Many Indian companies operate complex multi-tier structures or share operations with promoter entities.*

- [ ] **Legal Structure & Subsidiary Map:**
  | Entity Name | Relationship (Subsidiary / JV / Associate) | Ownership % | Operational Mandate / Segment | % of Cons. Revenue | % of Cons. PAT |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
- [ ] **Minority Interest & Upside Leakage:**
  - Does the core growth engine or highest-margin vertical reside in a partially owned subsidiary (e.g., 51% owned)?
  - Are there option agreements, profit-sharing pacts, or dilution clauses with joint-venture partners?
- [ ] **Overseas & Liaison Entities:**
  - What is the operational purpose of foreign entities (e.g., Frankfurt/Dubai/Singapore offices)? Are they genuine commercial marketing fronts, inventory warehousing hubs, or transfer-pricing / IP-holding vehicles?
- [ ] **Promoter Group Ecosystem & Parallel Businesses:**
  - Do the promoters own private, unlisted companies operating in identical, complementary, or adjacent lines of business?
  - Is there structural conflict of interest or brand sharing between the listed company and unlisted promoter entities?

## 3. The Operating Machine & Physical Assets

*Understand the physical assets, engineering capabilities, and statutory ceilings that govern the machine.*

- [ ] **Manufacturing & Operational Footprint:**
  - Map the primary plants, R&D labs, and logistics hubs: location, owned freehold land vs. leased, and strategic proximity to raw material sources vs. customer clusters.
- [ ] **Capacity, Throughput & Operating Headroom:**
  - State the nameplate installed capacity in physical units (tonnes, meters, pieces, hours).
  - What is the current capacity utilization (%)?
  - What is the peak achievable output before requiring a greenfield capex step-cliff? Can output be expanded 20%–30% via debottlenecking/brownfield additions?
- [ ] **Statutory & Environmental Ceilings (The Indian Operational Invariant):**
  - **Consent to Operate (CTO / CTE):** What is the consented production capacity granted by the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)? Is current production near the legal CTO cap?
  - Effluent Treatment & Water: Does the facility require Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) status? Are effluent treatment capacities or hazardous waste handling permissions constraining expansion?
  - Regulatory Certifications: Are facilities US FDA, EU GMP, IATF 16949, AS9100, or DIN certified? When are the next major audit cycles?
- [ ] **Proprietary Know-How, Tooling & Process IP:**
  - What is the proprietary core: custom tool & die room, specialized metallurgical heat-treatment recipes, proprietary chemical blending formulations, or custom algorithms?
  - How much of the process is automated high-speed machinery vs. manual shop-floor craftsmanship?
- [ ] **Operating Seams with Promoter Entities (RPT):**
  - Does the plant sit on land leased from promoters? Does the company rent offices, warehouses, or power infrastructure from promoter entities?

---

## 4. Supply Chain, Inputs & Pricing Pass-Through

*Where raw materials come from, and who bears commodity volatility.*

- [ ] **Key Raw Material Inputs & Exposure:**
  - What are the top 2–3 raw material inputs (e.g., specialized alloy steel, base oil, polyester yarn, copper, active chemical intermediates)?
  - Are inputs commodity items available from multiple domestic mills, or imported/specialty grades subject to international supply bottlenecks?
- [ ] **Pricing Pass-Through Architecture:**
  - Describe the contract mechanism: Fixed price, indexed cost-plus, or bilateral renegotiation.
  - Is there an explicit **Price Variation Clause (PVC)** linking selling prices to commodity indices?
  - **The Time-Lag:** How frequently does price reset (monthly, quarterly, bi-annually), and what is the lag time? (A 90-day lag means quarterly margin compression during sharp commodity spikes).
- [ ] **Supplier Concentration & Approved Manufacturer Lists (AML):**
  - Does the company depend on a single supplier for >25% of purchases?
  - Are raw material sources dictated by customer engineering drawings (e.g., global OEMs mandating specific European steel mills or approved chemical masterbatches)?
- [ ] **Import Dependency, Freight & Customs:**
  - What % of raw materials are imported (e.g., China, Europe)?
  - Exposure to basic customs duties, anti-dumping duties (ADD), ocean container freight spikes, and port congestion.

---

## 5. Customer Base, Empanelment & Concentration

*Who pays the company, how sticky they are, and where fragility hides.*

- [ ] **Customer Profile & Buyer Segmentation:**
  - Direct OEMs vs. Tier-1 integrators vs. regional distributors vs. EPC contractors vs. government agencies.
  - In Tier-1 arrangements, **is the payer the same as the decision-maker?** (e.g., does the auto OEM mandate the spring specification on the blueprint, leaving the Tier-1 assembler with zero authority to switch suppliers?).
- [ ] **Empanelment Barriers & Qualification Lead Times:**
  - What is required to win an initial commercial purchase order: Vendor code registration, First Article Inspection (FAI), PPAP Level 3/4 sign-off, plant audits?
  - How long does the pre-qualification cycle take (e.g., 6 months vs. 24 months)?
  - Once qualified, is the company single-source, dual-source (60:40 share), or one of five commodity vendors?
- [ ] **Concentration Mapping:**
  - Customer: Top 1 customer % and Top 10 customers % of revenue.
  - Geographic: Domestic vs. export split (and country breakdown).
  - Channel: Direct sales force vs. institutional distributors.
- [ ] **Export vs. Domestic Economics:**
  - How do realizations, product mix, and gross margins differ between domestic sales and exports?
  - Payment terms divergence: Domestic open credit (60–90 days) vs. export Letters of Credit (LC) / CAD.
  - Reliance on export incentive schemes (RoDTEP, Duty Drawback, EPCG duty-free capital imports).
- [ ] **Territorial & Licensing Export Restrictions (MNC / JV Ring-Fencing):**
  - Is the company legally, contractually, or via technology licensing agreements restricted from exporting to specific global territories (e.g., North America, Europe, East Asia)?
  - Does the foreign parent or JV partner legally restrict the Indian listed entity to India/SAARC while serving global markets from other parent-owned entities?
  - Can the company sell directly to global OEMs, or MUST all export sales route through parent-controlled overseas entities with parent-dictated transfer pricing?

## 6. Capital Requirements to Operate & Grow

*Describe the physical and working capital appetite of the business model. No ratio calculation required here — map the mechanics.*

- [ ] **Working Capital Dynamics (Who Funds the Cycle?):**
  - Does the company receive cash upfront/advances, or does it fund 60–120 days of customer credit?
  - Raw material inventory holding reality: Does the business require large buffer stocks (e.g., 90–180 days of imported steel/chemicals) due to minimum mill batch sizes, or can it operate JIT?
  - Creditor terms: Do raw material suppliers extend credit equal to, greater than, or significantly less than the customer receivables cycle?
- [ ] **Non-Fund-Based (NFB) Banking Line Intensity:**
  - Does the business model require heavy Bank Guarantees (Performance BGs, Advance BGs) or Letters of Credit (Inland/Import LCs) to bid for and execute orders?
  - Are banking limits and collateral availability an operational bottleneck to taking larger contracts?
- [ ] **Growth Capex Profile (Asset-Heavy vs. Asset-Light):**
  - To double revenue, what physical infrastructure must expand: Machinery, land, warehouses, headcount, or working capital alone?
  - Greenfield vs. Brownfield: Is future growth achievable via incremental machine additions inside existing factory sheds, or does it require greenfield land acquisition and utility setups?
- [ ] **Economic Capital Classification:**
  - **Cash Generator / Self-Funder:** Business model throws off cash as it scales (negative working capital, customer advances, low maintenance capex).
  - **Working Capital Absorber:** High gross margins on paper, but every ₹100 of incremental growth locks up ₹30–₹50 in receivables and raw inventory.
  - **Heavy Industrial Compounder:** Steady operating margins, but requires periodic large capex step-cliffs that temporarily depress cash flow.

## 7. Revenue Model, Visibility & Pricing Power

*How money comes in, how predictable it is, and what keeps gross margins intact.*

- [ ] **Nature of Revenue:**
  - *Contractual Recurring:* Active subscription / long-term take-or-pay contract with termination penalties.
  - *Repeating / Programmatic:* Regular rolling purchase orders issued under multi-year vendor codes for active OEM platforms.
  - *One-Off / Project Spot:* Every single order must be re-tendered and re-won (EPC contracts, turnkey plant installations).
- [ ] **Revenue Visibility & Backlog:**
  - On January 1, how much of the year's revenue is secured via confirmed firm order backlogs or long-term platform production schedules vs. what must be won from scratch?
  - Book-to-bill ratio and average order execution cycle time (weeks vs. multi-year projects).
- [ ] **Inherent Volatility & Seasonality:**
  - Is revenue inherently lumpy due to project milestone recognition (percentage-of-completion)?
  - Seasonal patterns: Monsoon dips (construction, mining, paint), festival spikes (textiles, retail), agricultural cycles (fertilizers, agrochemicals).
  - Commodity price linkage: Does top-line revenue swing wildly with underlying raw material prices even when physical volume is stable?
- [ ] **The "Explain the Gross Margin" Exercise:**
  - What structural forces keep the company's gross margin / gross conversion spread at its historical level?
  - What specific operational or market shift would cause gross margins to permanently expand by 500 bps?
  - What specific event would cause gross margins to compress by 500 bps?

## 8. The Industry Arena & Value Chain Economics

*Map the pond the fish swims in, where profit accumulates, and who holds the power.*

- [ ] **Define the Arena, Market Size & Headroom:**
  - What is the real game being played? Name the true market by the *job* delivered, not the company's marketing label.
  - **Market Sizing & Share:** What is the estimated market size (in ₹ Cr or $M) of the company's core segments, what is the company's approximate market share, and what is the underlying market volume growth rate? *(Note: If territorial or licensing export restrictions apply from Section 5, ensure the Serviceable Addressable Market reflects only legally accessible territories, not the theoretical global market).*
  - **Growth Runway:** Can the company double revenue simply by riding market growth, or does growth require taking market share from incumbents in a zero-sum battle?
  - If this company ceased operations tomorrow, exactly where would its customers go?
- [ ] **The Value Chain Economics Map:**
  - Trace the vertical chain:
    `Raw Material Extraction -> Primary Refining -> Specialized Component / Formulation -> Tier-1 Sub-System -> OEM Assembly -> End Customer`
  - Map typical operating margin and return profiles across each node. **Where does economic profit pool, and where is margin being compressed?**
  - Is there active margin migration along the chain (e.g., OEMs outsourcing more sub-assembly engineering upstream, or distributors being disintermediated)?
- [ ] **Industry Capacity & Utilization Dynamics:**
  - What is the estimated industry-wide capacity utilization in the company's primary product categories?
  - *Sellers' Market (>80%–85% utilization):* Disciplined pricing, stable-to-rising conversion margins, long customer lead times.
  - *Buyers' Market (<65%–70% utilization):* Idle overheads, aggressive discounting, unorganized price-cutting.
  - **The Capacity Pipeline:** What large greenfield or brownfield capacity expansions are currently under construction across the industry? Will supply additions outpace demand growth over the next 24 months?
- [ ] **Competitive Landscape (Named & Sized):**
  - Categorize and name direct rivals:
    1. *Organized Domestic Peers:* Who are they, and what are their relative scale and market shares?
    2. *Unorganized / Regional Micro-Clusters:* What percentage of the market is unorganized, and what tax, quality, or environmental factors are driving formalization?
    3. *Multinational / Global Incumbents:* European, American, or Japanese benchmark leaders.
    4. *Low-Cost Import Threats:* Threat from Chinese, Vietnamese, or other subsidized imports.
- [ ] **The Binding Competitive Force:**
  - Across Porter's Five Forces, identify the **single binding force** that places a ceiling on profitability for the average player in this industry.
- [ ] **Government Policy & Regulatory Regime:**
  - Is the industry protected by basic customs duties, Anti-Dumping Duties (ADD), or Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Quality Control Orders (QCOs)?
  - Is demand driven or subsidized by government schemes (PLI, PM Mitra, FAME, Smart Cities)? How durable is the policy support?

## 9. The Moat Hypothesis (Structural Source & Falsification)

*Formulate the structural case for why the business can sustain superior returns, and state exactly how Stage 3 will test it.*

- [ ] **Identify the Structural Source (Helmer's 7 Powers / Moat Archetypes):**
  - *Switching Costs:* Re-tooling costs, 12–24 month OEM audit recertification, mission-critical failure risk vs. low piece cost.
  - *Network Effects:* Multi-sided platform scale, liquidity or data advantages that expand with user count.
  - *Structural Cost Advantage:* Favorable geographic plant location, captive raw materials, continuous proprietary thermal furnace yield, scale amortization.
  - *Intangible Assets:* High-barrier regulatory licenses, statutory pollution permits (CTO) in restricted zones, patents, multi-decade brand trust in safety-critical parts.
  - *Efficient Scale:* Market size too small to justify a second scale competitor entering the niche.
  - *Counter-Positioning:* Operating model that incumbents cannot replicate without destroying their existing core revenues.
- [ ] **The "New Entrant Exercise":**
  - Assume a well-capitalized competitor enters with **₹500 Crore and 3 years**.
  - What would be trivially easy to replicate (e.g., purchasing standard CNC machines, leasing industrial sheds)?
  - What would be extremely difficult or impossible to replicate (e.g., 200 OEM vendor code approvals, customer trust in mission-critical applications, 10-year metallurgical testing libraries)?
  - Where exactly does the competitor get stuck?
- [ ] **Moat Width, Depth & Direction:**
  - *Width:* Does the advantage protect 80%+ of revenue, or only a small, high-margin specialty niche?
  - *Depth:* Quantify the structural moat buffer (e.g., 300–500 bps conversion spread advantage, 18-month switching hurdle).
  - *Direction:* Is the moat **widening** (strengthening flywheel, deeper OEM blueprint lock-in), **stable**, or **eroding** (technology substitution, unorganized commoditization, loss of key customer exclusivity)?
- [ ] **Rule Out the Moat Masqueraders:**
  - Is current high profitability mistaken for a moat when it is actually:
    - A temporary cyclical peak in global commodity margins?
    - A temporary government subsidy or protective tariff?
    - Fast industry volume growth floating all boats?
    - Just brilliant execution by current management that cannot outlive the promoter?
- [ ] **Falsification Conditions (Explicit Contract with Stage 3):**
  - Complete this sentence with concrete numerical thresholds:
    > *"If this structural moat hypothesis is true, Stage 3 financial forensics MUST confirm:  
    > 1. 10-year average ROIC consistently exceeds ____% (well above cost of capital), especially during the industry downturn of [Year].  
    > 2. Gross conversion spread / gross margin held within ±____% despite raw material inflation spikes in [Year].  
    > 3. Working capital days have remained stable or contracted, proving customers cannot stretch credit terms.  
    > **If these are NOT confirmed in the ledger, the moat hypothesis is FALSE.**"*

## 10. Adjacent Optionality & Expansion Runway

*Where can the company's existing capabilities naturally take it next?*

- [ ] **Natural "Right to Win" Adjacencies:**
  - What new product lines, chemistries, or end-market sectors can the company enter using its *existing* machinery, vendor codes, customer relationships, or metallurgical/formulation know-how?
  - Concrete examples of adjacent moves (e.g., precision spring maker entering green hydrogen electrolyzers; transformer oil refiner entering data center immersion fluids).
- [ ] **Reinvestment Runway:**
  - Is the company a **Static Fortress** (a highly defensible, high-ROIC cash cow in a mature niche that cannot absorb new capital, forced to pay out dividends)?
  - Or a **Compounding Machine** (large addressable market runway where cash can be redeployed into adjacent lines or export markets at high incremental ROIC)?

---

## 11. Stress Test: What Breaks the Business Model?

*Explicitly identify the structural failure points before moving forward.*

- [ ] **Scenario 1: Key Counterparty Loss:**
  - What happens if the largest customer or top vendor code is revoked or shifts to a competitor? How long to re-fill factory capacity?
- [ ] **Scenario 2: Sudden Input Shock:**
  - What happens if primary raw materials surge 40% in a single quarter? Does the pass-through protect absolute gross profit, or does working capital inflate and squeeze liquidity?
- [ ] **Scenario 3: Regulatory or Subsidy Sunset:**
  - If key pollution limits (CTO) are tightened, or import tariff protection (ADD) / export subsidies (RoDTEP) expire, what percentage of operating profit is eliminated?

## 13. Handoff to Subsequent Stages

*All non-business-model observations tagged `Fact → Stage N` collect here:*

- **→ Stage 2 (Management & Capital Allocation):**
  - Unlisted promoter group entity seams and RPT dependencies to audit.
  - Management guidance on capex, commissioning dates, and capacity ramp-ups to track in the historical say-do audit.
  - Promoter ownership, pledging, and executive remuneration alignment.
- **→ Stage 3 (Financial Forensics & Valuation):**
  - The explicit **Moat Falsification Conditions** to test in the 10-year ledger.
  - Cash conversion cycle, inventory buffer requirements, and working capital intensity to verify.
  - Non-Fund-Based (NFB) bank lines and contingent liability overhangs to quantify.
  - Normalized earnings power and reverse DCF implied growth ceiling.
- **→ Stage 4 (Thesis, Risks & Decision):**
  - The top structural breaking scenarios to integrate into the Pre-Mortem.
  - Customer/supplier single points of failure to translate into observable Kill Triggers.
  - Identified adjacent optionality as potential dated catalysts.
